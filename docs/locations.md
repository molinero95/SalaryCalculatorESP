# Residence presets and foral scope

Reviewed 9 October 2026. The selector covers all 17 autonomous communities. The Basque Country is split into Bizkaia, Gipuzkoa and Araba/Álava because they have separate tax administrations. Bilbao selects Bizkaia, Donostia/San Sebastián selects Gipuzkoa, Vitoria-Gasteiz selects Álava and Pamplona/Iruña selects Navarra. Existing common-regime community keys and saved scenarios remain compatible.

## Cities and residence selection

`js/data/cities.js` contains provincial capitals and a curated selection of major cities. It is not a complete municipal database or an automatically refreshed population ranking. The geographical reference is the [INE municipality/province catalogue, 1 January 2026](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/INEbase/operacion.htm?c=Estadistica_C&cid=1254736177031&idp=1254734710990). Display names include familiar bilingual forms; ids are stable application identifiers rather than INE codes.

The single **Tax residence** selector groups cities under their tax territory. Choose a city or the territory-only option for any municipality not listed. There are no separate city and community questions, salary preset buttons or municipal salary averages. Choosing a city changes residence only. It preserves salary, family details and simulations. Choosing a territory-only option clears the city. Imports discard city ids that do not match their region. Cities in the same tax jurisdiction use the same income-tax scale; no municipal IRPF rate is invented. The annual-scale view identifies the city → territory, common/foral regime, 2026 tax year and direct official-source links (including the 2026 Valencia/Extremadura amendments). It does not claim to include all regional deductions. Residence rules follow [AEAT habitual residence criteria](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/ayuda/manuales-videos-folletos/manuales-practicos/irpf-2025/c02-irpf-cuestiones-generales/sujecion-irpf-aspectos-personales/residencia-habitual-territorio-comunidad-autonoma.html) and [Bizkaia residence criteria](https://www.bizkaia.eus/ogasuna/faq/faq_detalle.asp?Estado=C&IdEtiquetaBuscar2=299&Idioma=CA&N1=1&Previsualizar=SI&Tem_Codigo=7120&Tem_CodigoPrev=5758&Version=1&btnFiltrar1=&btnFiltrar2=&idregistro=900002891&opttipo=&pant=listado); the selector does not adjudicate a change of residence or cross-territory employment.

The residence table shows the **current annual marginal scale**, including state + regional parts in the common regime and the full foral scale in foral territories. It is not a payroll withholding table or a copy of edited scenario brackets.

Ceuta and Melilla are autonomous cities, not autonomous communities. Their special relief and the La Palma relief remain outside the model. Do not add a generic common-regime city preset for them without modelling the relevant eligibility.

## Reviewed foral model

This first foral implementation supports individual assessment with a single full-year employment payer, permanent employment, age below 65, family situation 3, no descendants/ascendants/disability, no other income, no flexible compensation or pension contributions. It retains the existing social-security assumptions. Part-time salary is an entered annual amount; withholding uses that amount, not an imagined full-time salary.

The app explicitly hides payroll, charts, comparisons, breakdown and result-sharing actions for unreviewed foral profiles. The pure payroll entry point rejects those profiles instead of falling back to common-regime fiscal rules. Basic foral calculations never inherit the common €2,000 expense, common minima, common employment reduction or common low-wage credit.

The model assumes the withholding administration matches residence and workplace. Residence alone does not establish payroll withholding jurisdiction: cross-territory employment, public-service payers, remote work and changes during the year require a separate point-of-connection analysis. Basque/Navarre return results are estimates, not tax certification.

Common-regime income-tax editors, indexation and political income-tax proposals do **not** alter the fixed foral rules. The interface states this restriction. Social-security edits still work as hypothetical contribution scenarios. Extending hypothetical foral tax scales requires a separate scenario contract; do not silently reinterpret state-share proposals as foral laws.

Basque employment bonification: €8,000 through €14,800 net employment earnings; then €8,000 − 0.6098 × (net − €14,800) through €23,000; €3,000 above. It cannot exceed positive net employment earnings. The published formula gives €2,999.64 at exactly €23,000; do not replace it with an invented smooth endpoint.

Navarra has no common-regime employment base reduction. In the reviewed profile, article 64.5 income equals salary less employee SS. Article 62.5 work credit and article 62.9.a personal credit are quota deductions, separate from payroll withholding. Annual liability is nonnegative. The exclusive-employment filing exemption is **strictly below €17,000** in Navarra (article 56, DF 148/2025, effective 1 January 2026) and up to €20,000 inclusive in the Basque territories; profiles with other income or special mandatory filing rules are excluded.

## Independently worked examples

For €30,000 salary, 14 payments, permanent full-time employment and the basic profile, employee SS is €1,950. Basque withholding is 15% (€4,500). Net employment base is €30,000 − €1,950 − €3,000 = €25,050; annual quota is €4,158.40 + €6,970 × 28% = €6,110, less €1,615 = **€4,495**. Annual net after return is **€23,555**.

In Navarra the base is €28,050. The quota is €4,591.63 + €6,875 × 28% = €6,516.63. Credits: personal €1,084 + (€1,280 − 0.0904 × €10,550) = €1,410.28, and work €700. Annual tax is **€4,406.35**. Withholding is 14.6% (€4,380); annual net after return is **€23,643.65**.
